# Generative AI for Financial Forecasting

## Microsoft Case Study: ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini

### Overview

This project explores how **ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini** approach the same financial forecasting problem using Microsoft’s historical quarterly financial data.

The analysis focuses on **Revenue and COGS trends, gross profit forecasting, seasonality, margin behaviour, forecasting assumptions, and model interpretability**. Each LLM applied a different level of analytical and statistical sophistication, allowing their outputs to be compared from a **financial analyst’s perspective**.

The project also includes screenshots of the analysis, charts, and forecast visualizations generated by each model.

---

## Project Objective

The objective was to evaluate whether Generative AI can support financial analysts in:

* Analysing historical Revenue and COGS trends
* Identifying seasonality and margin behaviour
* Calculating historical Gross Profit
* Forecasting Gross Profit for the next four quarters
* Testing financial assumptions
* Comparing different forecasting methodologies
* Assessing model interpretability and limitations

The broader goal was not simply to identify the most advanced model, but to understand which approach provides the best balance of **financial reasoning, transparency, and analytical usefulness**.

---

## Financial Framework

The analysis was built around three key financial metrics:

### Gross Profit

`Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS`

### COGS as % of Revenue

`COGS % = COGS / Revenue`

### Gross Margin

`Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue`

Historical Revenue, COGS, Gross Profit, growth rates, and margin behaviour were analysed before forecasting future quarters.

---

## LLM Approaches

### ChatGPT

ChatGPT used a relatively straightforward and transparent forecasting approach.

It:

* Analysed historical Revenue and COGS trends
* Calculated growth and cost relationships
* Identified December-quarter seasonality
* Observed positive operating leverage at the gross profit level
* Used recent YoY growth trends to forecast Revenue and COGS
* Derived Gross Profit from the forecasted values

**Strength:** Clear, financially intuitive, and easy to validate.

**Limitation:** The forecast depends heavily on recent historical growth patterns continuing.

---

### Claude

Claude combined historical growth analysis with additional assumption testing.

It:

* Analysed Revenue and COGS growth
* Reviewed gross margin behaviour and seasonality
* Forecasted Revenue and COGS separately
* Applied recent YoY growth trends
* Tested an additional trend-based scenario
* Assessed whether projected margins remained financially reasonable

A key observation was that trend extrapolation could push gross margins to approximately **72% to 74%**, above recent historical levels.

**Strength:** Strong assumption testing and financial reasonableness checks.

**Limitation:** Trend extrapolation may produce overly optimistic forecasts when recent improvement is extended too far.

---

### Gemini

Gemini used the most quantitatively detailed forecasting framework.

The analysis incorporated:

1. **Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing**
2. **SARIMA**
3. **YoY Growth Momentum**
4. **Linear Trend with Seasonal Dummies**
5. **Margin-Based Model**
6. **Ensemble Consensus**

The ensemble approach combined multiple model outputs rather than relying on a single forecasting technique.

**Strength:** Greater quantitative depth and broader treatment of trend and seasonality.

**Limitation:** Higher complexity reduces transparency and makes the output more difficult to independently validate.

## Key Findings

### 1. Revenue Growth Has Generally Outpaced COGS Growth

Microsoft’s historical Revenue has generally grown faster than COGS.

This indicates improving cost efficiency and supports a **positive gross margin trend**, with margins broadly around the 70% to 72% range in the forecast analysis.

From a financial analyst perspective, this suggests positive operating leverage at the gross profit level.

However, this relationship should not automatically be assumed to continue. Cost structure, competition, business mix, and AI-related infrastructure investment could affect future margins.

---

### 2. Seasonality Matters in Quarterly Forecasting

Recurring December-quarter patterns were identified in Microsoft’s historical results.

This highlights why quarterly financial performance should be assessed using both:

* Sequential growth
* Year-on-year growth
* Comparison with the same quarter in previous years

Ignoring seasonality can result in misleading conclusions about underlying business performance.

---

### 3. Forecasting Sophistication Varied Significantly

The three LLMs approached the same dataset very differently.

**ChatGPT:** Simple and transparent growth-based forecasting

**Claude:** Growth-based forecasting with additional scenario testing

**Gemini:** Multiple statistical models combined through an ensemble approach

This demonstrates an important forecasting trade-off.

Greater sophistication can increase analytical depth, but it can also make the model harder to understand, explain, and validate.

---

### 4. Financial Reasonableness Matters

A statistically valid forecast is not automatically a financially reasonable forecast.

Claude’s trend-based scenario demonstrated this by producing gross margins above recent historical levels.

A financial analyst should therefore test forecast outputs against:

* Historical performance
* Business economics
* Cost structure
* Margin behaviour
* Recent operating trends

AI-generated outputs should be challenged before being accepted.

---

### 5. Interpretability Is Important

Financial forecasts often need to be communicated to senior management, investors, clients, or other stakeholders.

For this reason, an analyst must understand:

* What assumptions drive the forecast
* How the forecast was calculated
* Why the result is financially reasonable
* What risks could cause the forecast to change

ChatGPT and Claude were easier to interpret, while Gemini provided greater statistical depth at the cost of transparency.

---

### 6. Every Model Carries Model Risk

Each approach introduced a different form of forecasting risk.

**ChatGPT:** Dependence on continued historical growth

**Claude:** Risk of overextending recent trends

**Gemini:** Greater complexity and reduced transparency

The best forecasting approach is therefore not necessarily the most sophisticated one.

It is the approach that is **financially reasonable, explainable, and fit for purpose**.

---

## Financial Analyst Perspective

The project highlights an important distinction between **generating a forecast and analysing a forecast**.

Generative AI can quickly identify trends, calculate financial relationships, generate scenarios, and apply statistical forecasting techniques. However, the analyst still needs to assess whether the output makes sense within the context of the business.

A strong financial forecast should therefore satisfy four tests:

**Financial Logic → Historical Consistency → Interpretability → Forecast Accuracy**

Generative AI is most effective when used as an analytical support tool rather than as a replacement for financial judgement.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how three leading Generative AI models can produce substantially different financial forecasting approaches from the same underlying dataset.

**ChatGPT** provided the most transparent and intuitive approach.

**Claude** added value through stronger assumption testing and financial reasonableness checks.

**Gemini** provided the greatest quantitative depth through multiple statistical models and ensemble forecasting.

The key takeaway is that **model sophistication alone does not determine forecast quality**. Effective financial forecasting requires a combination of quantitative analysis, transparent assumptions, financial judgement, and independent validation.

Generative AI can significantly improve the speed and breadth of financial analysis, but the analyst remains responsible for understanding the assumptions, challenging the results, and determining whether the forecast is financially reasonable.
